Reading Reflections

On Photography by Susan Sontag

Photography is something that has changed the ways we see our day to day, it has been used by all of us to retain a moment by others to inform others and even to share part of our life with strangers, throughout the reading “On Photography” there is a clear depiction on the different perspectives of photography society has had following the use of photography to capture what us as humans find interesting while also focusing on how picture taking later evolved into manipulating the world and what we want others to see of it. Creativity can be used freely and reflected on a piece of work for others to also enjoy while also commemorating a memory via an image and how these can cause a remembrance or serve as a memento for a group of people or for yourself as mentioned on Photography by Susan, “To collect photographs is to collect the world”. These images or videos are embedded with different memories by each individual that was part of building that part of the past which makes it very special and interesting to look back on and share. The different stories, interpretation and point of view can be told simply from a photograph or video. Throughout the reading Susan Sontag also offers a fascinating perspective as to why certain people may have more interest in taking lots of photographs and “documenting” the moment to save for later potentially due to being “robbed of their past [making them more fervent picture takers at home and abroad]” (pg. 10, paragraph 2). This perspective ties back to the reasoning behind picture taking and what pictures and images represent to every particular person or group of people being that shared experiences in the past leads them to have such depth and passion for photography in comparison to others. Additionally, Sontag makes remarks on pictures taken with an intention to hypothetically make a change in the publics/societies moral position, with this, the reactions and changes they were seeking were not met but instead help create a new position or attitude towards an already going matter (pg. 17, paragraph 2). Colors and different angles can create/manifest a reaction by transmitting or attaching personal emotions to the viewer along with clear reasoning as to why this piece of work was included/taken rather than causing confusion or misunderstandings, but pictures and videos of past tense events sort of serve as a memorable piece of work rather than something that moves a person to seek change or opinions. In the beginning use of photography, the person being shown a picture was not present during the event or had any way to compare the actions being displayed in it, in other words form a connection to their personal life to feel or any personal context, then the action of an image to them would be simply and usually solely something “of the same order as fiction”(pg. 22, paragraph 1). Moreover, nowadays with constant use of images on billboards, websites, social media, books, articles, news, etc these are now used to lure the public into more written information under rather than just admiring a photo or letting the viewer get to its own personal conclusion of the image without more information to arguably change the person's perspective or understanding on a subject.

Motherhood in Crisis by Laurence Ivil, Alicia Prager and Saidu Bah

The motherhood story titled, “My Stolen Future” from the set of stories of Motherhood in Crisis stood out to me. Throughout this story there are various uses of animation along with a mixture of real life recordings of people, in this case, a Neneh Conteh, counsellor of the Rainbo Centre. The Rainbo centre is introduced to the viewer after following Heela in decision making on her future and her experience being part of this centre due to the circumstances she was now undergoing. Heela mentions how she felt safe and cared for in this centre in part because she was receiving the attention that her community did not receive health wise, but also physically with food, as well as a group of people who, like her, were also undergoing difficult circumstances, because of this she felt in a safe place to confide in them. Illustrations, compared to photography, evoke more emotion towards the story while simultaneously controlling the narrative, more precisely, by explaining, drawing attention to or from something, therefore the viewer does not reach erroneous conclusions or interpret something differently than how another person would, it is a clear message all around. Additionally, illustrations perform the function of simplifying information, usually complex, that a photograph, containing overly detailed elements or having a narrow control over what is being photographed, may contain. The person creating the illustrations has complete control over what is being displayed, what the viewer will understand and the emotions connected within it. In using an illustrative style that combines text, illustration, photography and video there is a better understanding of Heela’s feelings throughout all of this by giving the viewer a sense of being with Heela and following along her story with her while also knowing what happened to her. The use of dialogue allows the viewer to feel present as these moments were happening while reading along with them. The illustration aids in transmitting the feelings Heela was facing throughout these difficult times, in the showcasing of a frown, sadness with her head down, frustration and loneliness when being forced to do something she does not want to do, and finally worry by not understanding what was going on with her body and residing in her friend trusting her to explain what was happening and later acceptance of her new life, now leading into another stage of decision making. The advantages of these forms of story telling is the communication of emotions throughout, so there is a larger emotional impact to the viewer resulting in more empathy towards the person's story being told. By communicating via a mix of text, illustration, photography and video the viewer will then retain the information for a longer period of time and understand the message trying to be disseminated to the target group of people. Including a video amongst the animations/illustrations work as an eye opener that this is a real ongoing issue and it is something that is still occurring, hence the need to vocalize Heela’s story and the existence of centres like that of Rainbo.

Your Brain on Chat GPT

Throughout the reading Nataliya Kosmyna guides us, the reader, through a study that is specifically focusing to look at the detrimental effects of LLM also known as AI platforms such as Chat GPT and OpenAI. A brief explanation as to the reasons and basis of this study is mentioned to be due to the ongoing increase of the vast dependency of the majority of businesses and how the dependency of these systems have a long lasting effects on the brain. The study took place by "[recruiting] a total of 54 participants for Sessions 1, 2, 3, and 18 participants among them completed session 4". For the sessions 1,2,3 these participants were fit into different groups "LLM group, Search Engine group, and Brain-only group, where each participant used a designated tool (or no tool in the latter) to write an essay". The results of this study based on each participant reflect the neural connectivity pattern changes LLM systems versus non caused to their brains. The results were pretty much continuous, with the groups who used none to all help from LLM systems there was a large gap of brain connectivity to their work, lacking the ability to recall information and being truly engaged to it. Kosmyna touches on some points this study has helped bring attention to in terms of its effects on the population when speaking on the harmful effects LLM models have on the cognitive and practical learning of the public. For instance, throughout the study the main attention was put into how these LLM models affect a learning outcome looking specifically for patterns in that field rather than how these patterns can furtherly affect other tasks a person completes outside an educational stand point. Additionally, this reading admits to not being able to form a concrete stance on the lifelong effects of ChatGPT and other AI operated models stating that for it to be possible "exploring longitudinal impacts of tool usage on memory retention, creativity, and writing fluency" for future studies should be considered. Following this perspective, the video titled "Brain Experts WARNING: Watch This Before Using ChatGPT Again!" go more in depth into how these models are causing and can potentially increase damage to brain activity, along with different mental disorders, what's healthy for your brain, following what can be the causes of the rise in many of these executive dysfunctions and neurodivergences by mentioning how about 47% of people who used ChatGPT or another AI tool had very little brain activity while completing their task and a bigger percentage of them could not recall information they were actively writing about. Later throughout the video, the later consequences of dependency and addiction of AI to complete a task is becoming more and more normal, these early signs can persist and since the person, as proofed in many studies, no longer has the capacity to perform a task without needing or relying solely on the information being presented to them by these LLM models, results in a detachment to what they are "learning" at the moment which can later result in long term health issues relating to dementia, alzheimer's and other.
